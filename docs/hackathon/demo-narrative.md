# Permit — Three-Minute Demo Narrative

## Core Message

AI agents are becoming economic actors. Permit gives them a safe way to act: humans define the boundary, agents operate inside it, and PayPal-backed evidence proves what happened.

## Six-Beat Demo

### 1. Grant authority

Show a human creating a Permit:

- USD 50 maximum
- 24-hour expiry
- Approved merchant or purchase scope
- Evidence requirement enabled

**Say:** “The human defines exactly what the agent may do—and what it may never do.”

### 2. Agent proposes

Show the AI agent receiving an intent and returning a structured proposal:

- Item or service
- Merchant
- Amount
- Rationale
- Permit ID

**Say:** “The agent can research and propose, but it cannot silently expand its own authority.”

### 3. Policy authorizes

Show Permit checking:

- Remaining cap
- Approved merchant
- Expiry
- Revocation status
- Duplicate protection

**Say:** “Authorization is deterministic. The AI does not decide whether it may spend.”

### 4. PayPal captures

Show the approved proposal completing through PayPal sandbox.

**Say:** “The approved action executes through PayPal, with idempotency protection against duplicate captures.”

### 5. Evidence is written

Show the receipt ledger:

- Proposal
- Authorization decision
- PayPal result
- Timestamp
- Agent identity
- Hash chain

**Say:** “Every meaningful action produces verifiable evidence.”

### 6. Human retains control

Show:

- A USD 75 attempt blocked under the USD 50 Permit
- Emergency revoke
- A later attempt denied
- The denial recorded in the ledger

**Say:** “If something looks wrong, the human can stop it immediately—and Permit proves both the action and the stop.”

- [ ] Closing: honest prototype boundary and production roadmap
- [ ] Total runtime under three minutes
- [ ] No secrets, tokens, personal data, or live-account details visible

## Claims Policy

Say only what the demo shows.

- “PayPal sandbox” — not “production PayPal”
- “Prototype evidence ledger” — not “blockchain-grade security”
- “Demonstrated control” — not “guaranteed protection against every attack”
